MICHIGAN / ELIGIBILITY LEARNING CENTER

Understand the category. See the learner.

Explore all 13 federal IDEA disability categories and Michigan terminology. Use these guides to support instruction and collaboration, never to diagnose or decide eligibility.

Characteristics ≠ diagnosis ≠ educational eligibility

A characteristic is an observation. A medical diagnosis is a clinical determination. IDEA eligibility requires an individualized evaluation and a determination that a child meets applicable criteria and, because of the disability, needs special education and related services. The parent and qualified professionals make the eligibility determination; this website cannot do so.

No single measure decides eligibility. Evaluation considers multiple sources, educational needs, appropriate instruction, and English-language proficiency. Follow your school’s evaluation procedures; do not use interventions to delay a required evaluation.

Michigan terminology compared with the 13 federal categories
Terminology guide; categories are not always one-to-one equivalents.
Federal IDEA categoryMichigan terminologyMARSE rule

Federal developmental delay is an optional, age-limited provision under §300.8(b), separate from the 13 categories in §300.8(c). Michigan early childhood developmental delay applies through age seven under R 340.1711; it is not a general middle school category. Michigan severe multiple impairment does not map exactly to the federal multiple-disabilities definition.

SPECIFIC LEARNING DISABILITIES / SKILL BY SKILL

Name the skill. Match the support.

Dyslexia, dysgraphia, and dyscalculia are descriptive terms, not separate federal eligibility categories. Explore how specific reading, writing, and mathematics needs differ.

Descriptive terms, evaluation areas, and eligibility: what is the difference?

IDEA’s SLD definition explicitly includes dyslexia. Dysgraphia and dyscalculia are commonly used descriptive terms, with meanings that may vary. They do not automatically establish SLD eligibility.

§300.309 identifies eight SLD academic areas: oral expression, listening comprehension, written expression, basic reading skills, reading fluency skills, reading comprehension, mathematics calculation, and mathematics problem solving. This center focuses on the requested reading, writing, and mathematics topics; it is not the complete evaluation framework.

The team must consider the full evaluation criteria, appropriate instruction, exclusionary factors, and the need for special education. A screening, classroom behavior, or diagnosis is not an eligibility decision. Learning disabilities can coexist with other needs; overlapping concerns require individualized evaluation.

01 / ACCOMMODATION TOOLKIT

Remove barriers. Keep the learning.

Accommodations change how students access learning or demonstrate understanding. Follow each student’s IEP or Section 504 plan and collaborate with the case manager.

◫
Accommodations versus modifications — and following the IEP

An accommodation changes access or how learning is demonstrated without changing the intended learning expectation. A modification changes what the learner is expected to learn or demonstrate. The same tool can function differently depending on the task: a calculator can support reasoning access but change a calculation assessment.

Implement each student’s current IEP as written. Required supports are not rewards and should not be removed because a student did not ask. Consult the case manager about ambiguity, assessment rules, or barriers, and use the team’s process for changes. General classroom strategies do not replace required specially designed instruction.
